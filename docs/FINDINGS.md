# Findings

**Status: preliminary. One 9B hybrid model for most results, one 33B model at one length, one prompt per cell, no end-task accuracy.**
Everything here is read from machine-written JSON under `results/` (linked below). The numbers measure how well a key-retrieval index
matches true attention. They are not accuracy measurements and do not reproduce the paper's claims. Where a result is unflattering to the
method it is stated as such. The method and reference code belong to Prefrontal AI (arXiv:2610.42147). This is independent exploration.

## What was tested

The paper's **training-free** variant (Latent Sparse Attention with PCA index projections, flat and tree search, see [paper-notes.md](paper-notes.md)),
on the full-attention layers of Ornith 1.5 9B (a Qwen3.5-family hybrid, 8 of 32 layers) at 32K, 128K, 512K and 917K tokens, and Nanbeige4.3-33B
(plain Llama, all 52 layers) at 32K. Prompts are coherent text from one source each: essays, one code repository, one documentation set.
**Retrieval efficiency** is the captured head-averaged attention mass outside the sink and window, divided by the exact top-k at the same
selection size ([metrics.md](metrics.md)). Controls matched in size: random, most recent keys, exact top-k, and the paper's own baseline family
(block-sparse, blocks of 4 keys scored on raw keys). 16 queries from the end of each prompt. Dumps beyond 262K tokens use YaRN x4, so the model's
attention there is out of distribution.

### 1. Retrieval beats random and, mostly, recency, but reaches only about half of the ideal at small budgets
At k=2048 the mean efficiency of flat retrieval is 0.43 to 0.56 for prose and code at every length from 32K to 917K, and 0.83 then 0.64 for the technical docs at 32K then 128K.
Random selection captures a few percent. Flat retrieval beats plain recency on most layers in most cells, but not all: recency wins on the layers that are mostly local
(layers 3 and 7 of Ornith), and on the 512K code prompt and the 128K technical docs it overtakes flat retrieval at k>=4096.

### 2. No collapse out to 917K tokens (0.22% of keys), with caveats
Efficiency at a fixed k does not fall steadily with context (prose 0.53, 0.43, 0.50 at 32K, 128K, 512K; code 0.54, 0.56, 0.46, 0.56 at 32K, 128K, 512K, 917K).
The prompts differ between lengths (the 128K prose prompt is a different essay assembly from the 512K one, the code prompts are different slices of one repository,
and the 512K and 917K code prompts are much more repetitive: 8-gram repetition 0.19 and 0.27 against about 0.10 below 131K). So this is "no sharp collapse", not a clean trend.

### 3. Genre matters more than length
Technical documentation at 32K reaches 0.83 at k=2048 (7 of 8 layers at or above 0.70), prose and code about 0.53. The documentation set is structured and partly repetitive,
which probably makes keys easier to find. This is one documentation repository, not a general claim about technical text.

### 4. The tree is indistinguishable from the flat search in the mean, in every run, to 917K tokens
Mean efficiency of tree and flat agree to two decimals in every cell. On Ornith the largest single-layer difference over all runs is 0.006 (ornith-tech131072 k=4096); on Nanbeige it reaches 0.05 (k=512, 32K, scaled tree settings).
That includes 512K and 917K with the upstream tree settings (top level 16, node bucket 4, beam 2048). The hierarchy loses nothing measurable in retrieval quality, which is consistent with the paper,
where the tree exists for cost, not quality. At 128K and below the beam is wide relative to the candidate set, so little or no pruning happens and the two are expected to agree.

### 5. Block-sparse is as good as LSA on Ornith and clearly better on Nanbeige
On Ornith, block-sparse (which scores every block of 4 keys, at higher index cost) is ahead of flat LSA by at most 0.033 in the mean (largest in prose at 32K, k=512), and often by about 0.01. On Nanbeige at 32K it is ahead by 0.07
(0.62 against 0.55 at k=2048, ahead on 51 of 52 layers). Nanbeige rotates every head dimension, so the paper's recipe (position-free dims) cannot be followed: our index covers all post-RoPE dims, which is
off-recipe. Widening the index from 64 to 1024 dims moves flat from 0.55 to 0.59, so about half of the gap is the projection and the rest is bucket granularity (about 32 keys against blocks of 4).

### 7. Pre-registered gates
Written before the paper-mode sweep ([plan.md](plan.md)). Outcomes, not edited after the fact:
- **Gate 1 (retrieval quality at 32K, Ornith):** efficiency at or above 0.70 on at least half the layers at k<=2048, and beating recency and block-sparse on at least half. **Fails on prose (0 of 8 layers) and code (0 of 8),
  passes on the technical docs (7 of 8 layers at or above 0.70; flat beats recency on 8 of 8 and block-sparse on 4 of 8, exactly at the threshold).** On Nanbeige it fails (0 of 52 layers at or above 0.70, block-sparse ahead).
  My own second criterion (beat block-sparse) was mis-specified, since the paper claims parity at a cheaper index, not better retrieval. The original wording stays on record.
- **Gate 2 (cost on the R9700):** centroid scoring plus gather cheaper than exhaustive scoring at 32K and 128K. **Not met as written**: at 32K and 128K exhaustive scoring alone (0.17 and 0.29 ms) costs far less than centroid
  scoring plus the 2.2 ms gather. Against exhaustive scoring plus the same gather the advantage is about 1.1x at 128K, 1.2x at 1M and 2.8x at 10M. Clustering was timed with the CPU reference only.

## What this does not show
- Anything about **end-task accuracy**. The paper measures accuracy (RULER up to 10M tokens and others) with distilled indexers on H200 and B300 GPUs. We measure attention-mass retrieval of a training-free index. High or low mass does not
  by itself say whether a model's answers survive.
- Anything at **10M tokens** for the real model: its KV cache does not fit on hardware available here. The 10M figures are synthetic cost numbers only.
- Behaviour at 512K and 917K for a **model in distribution**: those dumps use YaRN x4 on a model with a 262K native context.
- Generality across models: the long runs are all one 9B hybrid, plus one 33B dense model at 32K and one genre.

## Limitations of the experiments
Queries are only from the last 16 positions of each prompt (decode-like), one prompt per cell, and the PCA basis is fitted on the same prompt it serves (the cross-prompt option `--pca-from` exists but was not run).
Prompts differ across lengths and genres in repetition and source, so cross-length comparisons are indicative. The window is 512 keys at every length, 12.5% of the 4K-scale contexts and negligible at 917K.
Clustering uses the reference implementation, not a GPU kernel. The first probe (experiment 000) is superseded: it counted the window mass and did not follow the paper's index recipe.

## Engineering notes that may help others
- Hugging Face's sdpa path expands K and V to the query-head count whenever a mask is present, which does not fit beyond roughly 450K tokens for this model (7.6 GiB per layer at 500K). `src/lsa_rocm/dump.py --gqa-sdpa` folds each
  KV head's query group into the row dimension instead and was validated against the single-pass dump (cosine at or above 0.9998). With it, a 917K-token forward pass peaks at 26.5 GiB on a 32 GiB card.
- `--kv-offload` (CPU-resident cache), `--prefill-chunk`, `--rope-yarn` and `--offload-weights-gib` are in the same file. Every dump carries an independent self-check: the last query's attention recomputed from the saved Q, K, V against the module's own output (cosine above 0.99999 in every run).
- LLM agents (DeepSeek, then LongCat as fallback) did much of the implementation. They failed often on network errors, permission denials and over-broad tasks; their code needed review and several runs were finished or fixed by hand.
  Acceptance was by supervisor-written scripts that recompute results independently. The reports are in `agents/reports/`.
